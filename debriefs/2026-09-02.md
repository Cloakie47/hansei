## Step 2 — What went wrong (two worst decisions)

Candidates weighed: the v2 clustering ship, the packet-id collision, the
BREAKOUT classifier accepting a 13%-down asset, and the chase-manufacture
that produced today's two rejections. The two below are the genuinely
worst — one for what it did, one for what it says about how I verify.
(The 0G misclassification, for the record, failed SAFE: the mislabeled
candidate was still suppressed by R014's 0.54:1, so no packet resulted;
that is why it is not in the top two. The chase-manufacture is charged to
day 1's spec and already headlined there.)

**1. The id collision that SILENTLY OVERWROTE a packet.**
- What and why: I used p-...-998/999 as synthetic test ids in the real
  id sequence. The day counter passed 999, the allocator's \d{3} pattern
  went blind to 4-digit ids, and the next two packets both received
  p-20260901-1000 — the second overwrote the first with no error.
- What happened: caught only because two identical paths printed in the
  scan output; the overwritten packet was never presented and was
  regenerated after the fix.
- The specific error: two compounding choices — polluting a production
  sequence with test values, and writing a pattern that assumed a bound
  nothing enforced. In a product whose entire claim is record integrity,
  a silent artifact overwrite is the worst failure class available, and
  it was self-inflicted. That it destroyed only a not-yet-presented packet
  was luck, not design.
- First time or repeat: first time. Fix is structural (\d{3,}), and test
  entries now use flagged verdicts rather than sequence-consuming ids.

**2. Shipping v2 with structural clustering just above its own floor.**
- What and why: flat 0.62 base meant ordinary two-source candidates landed
  at 0.610-0.638 — clearing the 60% floor by construction, the exact
  pattern the anti-gaming suppression log exists to expose.
- What happened: the confounding check caught it within hours; the Pilot
  approved the tier-base fix (0.58/0.62); re-score dropped floor-clearers
  from 10/10 to 1/10.
- The specific error: I verified v2 against its spec endpoints (70%+ on
  strong confluence, <=80% cap) but never against the distribution it
  would actually produce. Testing the corners and not the middle is how a
  formula passes review while defeating the gate it feeds.
- First time or repeat: first time — and the v3 rollout inherits the
  lesson: it ships in shadow with a mandatory distribution check on a
  quiet day before promotion.

is with timestamps.** The packets were generated 2026-09-01 ~19:57Z (FIL
+15.0%, CRV +15.2%, both near range highs, both passing every gate then in

the code caught up nine minutes later and then agreed. That is the

not SIZE, not TIMING, not ASSET — both times. WHAT THEY DO NOT REVEAL:
anything about what the Pilot APPROVES (there are no approvals to compare
against), whether setup-classified packets will fare better, or any
directional/asset preference. Two rejections of near-identical packets is
one data point about one packet shape, not a pattern.
