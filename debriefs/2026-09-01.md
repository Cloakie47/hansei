## Step 2 — What went wrong (two worst decisions)

**1. Shipping R005 with ticker-string matching.**
- What and why: the ingest filter accepted any signal whose ticker mapped to a
  listed USDT pair, because the ticker was the only key both universes shared.
- What happened: a BSC token calling itself WIN (120 USDT liquidity, risk
  level 4, zero volume) passed R005 and reached the candidate stage
  masquerading as WINkLink before the numbers exposed it.
- The specific error: I keyed identity on a mutable, collision-prone string
  instead of the one immutable identifier available (contract address). Not a
  data problem — a design decision that optimised for easy matching over
  correctness.
- First time or repeat: first time. R006 now closes it fail-closed.

**2. Presenting two coin-flip packets (54%, 55%) on the same afternoon.**
- What and why: after the on-chain feeds measured 0 eligible/day, the first
  CEX-native scan produced candidates and I packaged the top two immediately —
  UNI long at 85% of its range against an ask-heavy book, DASH knife-catch on
  one book snapshot.
- What happened: both sat unanswered; the Pilot's same-day response was to
  mandate a LOW CONVICTION flag for anything under 60%.
- The specific error: my own spec says "if you have no idea that clears the
  bar, say No proposal today — do not manufacture activity." I let "the
  pipeline finally works" substitute for "the edge is real." A 54% confidence
  number is an admission that I do not have a thesis, dressed as a proposal.
- First time or repeat: first time as packets — but the test packet
  p-20260901-002 drew the same criticism ("55% is a coin flip dressed up") in
  its simulated rejection hours earlier. So: a repeat of a flagged pattern
  within one day. That is the headline of this Debrief.

below as R009.
